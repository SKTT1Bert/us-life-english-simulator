# Contemporary conversational English

The twelve records in `data/trendingExpressions.js` add a small, curated set of informal expressions for university friends, social plans, and conversations about online content. Ten are practical conversational candidates. **Aura farming** and **lowkenuinely** are explicitly marked as optional internet-meme vocabulary; they are useful for recognition and playful contexts rather than general-purpose substitutes for ordinary English.

Meanings and register notes were checked against the primary references below on **2026-10-22**. This is not a ranked popularity list, a representative survey of US students, or a claim that all twelve phrases originated in 2025–2026. Dictionary recognition and dated usage support the choices, while familiarity still varies by person and community. In particular, low-key, bet, slaps, ate, and let them cook have established histories. Several expressions grew out of Black American or LGBTQ+ communities; their inclusion does not treat those speech varieties as a recent internet invention.

All English teaching examples, Chinese translations, usage notes, and neutral alternatives were authored for this project. They are not copied dictionary examples. `source_url` points to the reference for meaning; `verified_at` records our check date, not the word's creation date or a measurement of current popularity. The B1/B2 labels are editorial learning suggestions based on the examples, not validated CEFR classifications of slang.

| Expression | Reference and time evidence |
| --- | --- |
| I'm cooked. | [Merriam-Webster: cooked](https://www.merriam-webster.com/slang/cooked) includes 2025 examples and explains that the predicament sense is much older. [Cambridge: cooked](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/cooked) also distinguishes the exhausted sense. |
| I need to lock in. | [American Dialect Society's January 2025 report](https://americandialect.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/2024-Word-of-the-Year-PRESS-RELEASE.pdf) selected lock in as its Most Useful expression for 2024. This dates recognition of the focused-attention sense, not its invention. |
| Let them cook. | [Dictionary.com: let him cook](https://www.dictionary.com/culture/slang/let-him-cook), published in 2023, explicitly includes them/her variants and older usage. Treat as established slang. |
| I low-key want to try it. | [Cambridge: low-key](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/low-key), current entry checked on the date above. The adverb softens how an opinion is presented; it does not always mean secret or weak. |
| It's giving cozy café vibes. | [BBC Learning English's April 2025 lesson](https://downloads.bbc.co.uk/learningenglish/features/tews/250428_tews_its_giving_transcript.pdf) teaches this style/mood construction and describes its spread into popular culture. |
| You ate! | [Merriam-Webster: ate and left no crumbs](https://www.merriam-webster.com/slang/ate-and-left-no-crumbs) explains the praise sense and ballroom origins. The [January 2025 ADS report](https://americandialect.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/2024-Word-of-the-Year-PRESS-RELEASE.pdf) also lists the performance sense among its 2024 nominations. |
| We went on a little side quest. | [Dictionary.com: side quest](https://www.dictionary.com/browse/side-quest) includes the secondary-pursuit sense and 2026 examples outside games. |
| That looks like rage bait. | [Oxford's 2025 Word of the Year announcement](https://corp.oup.com/news/the-oxford-word-of-the-year-2025-is-rage-bait/) identifies rage bait as its winner and reports a threefold increase in use over that year. This does not mean every controversial post is intentionally baiting people. |
| Bet. I'll be there. | [Merriam-Webster: bet](https://www.merriam-webster.com/slang/bet), current entry checked on the date above, distinguishes agreement, challenge, and sarcastic senses. Its origins predate contemporary social media. |
| This song slaps. | [Dictionary.com's slang guide](https://www.dictionary.com/articles/gen-z-slang), published in 2022, explains the approving musical use and its earlier history. Included as established useful slang, not a newly emerging 2026 expression. |
| It's just a bit of aura farming. | [Oxford's 2025 press kit](https://corp.oup.com/oxford-word-of-the-year-2025-press-kit/) describes a rise in usage and a July 2025 peak. The record uses self-directed humor to avoid teaching an insult as a default social response. |
| I lowkenuinely needed that break. | [Merriam-Webster: lowkenuinely](https://www.merriam-webster.com/slang/lowkenuinely), updated 2026-05-11, places its emergence in late 2025 and gives dated 2025/2026 uses. The blend is less broadly familiar than its neutral alternative, genuinely. |

All twelve records support the same study, favorite, recall, and export behavior as other built-in content. The two optional items additionally carry `meme` and `optional` tags so their usage guidance remains explicit. IDs use a stable `slang-` prefix and are separate from user-authored `custom:` UUIDs. The data module publishes a frozen `window.TRENDING_EXPRESSIONS` array and does not append to or modify `window.EXPRESSIONS` itself. Text is ordinary learning material; metadata links are references, not model-generated answers or verified proficiency results.
